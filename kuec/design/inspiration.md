# Inspiration: week 1 plan

The Inspiration phase of the IDEO method applied to the KUEC BD team. It runs in the week of 2026-10-26 in parallel with the foundations. Nothing in this file is a finding; findings go to `journal.md` the same day they are heard. The research protocol itself (session guide, top-tasks exercise, card sort, tree test) is in `research.md` section 8 and is not repeated here.

## 1. Frame the challenge

The How-might-we statements HMW1 to HMW5 in `journal.md`. Each session is tagged with the statements it informs. A statement with no evidence after eight sessions is dropped or rewritten in synthesis.

## 2. Who we learn from: extremes and mainstreams

| Participant | Why this person | HMW | Session |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The ED | The most experienced; decides every gate; currently chases | HMW2 | The hour before and after a Thursday review, plus one escalation handled this month |
| The newest BD officer | The first-week perspective; has not learned the workarounds | HMW1, HMW4 | Logging a lead from a business card; the morning after a site visit |
| A reluctant Commercialization colleague | Will use the CRM only if it costs nothing to learn | HMW3 | Receiving and delivering a piece of work handed over by BD, from the email onwards |
| The mobile-only field officer | Works between meetings on a phone | HMW4 | Returning from a site visit; what happens to the three things they promised |
| The ops coordinator | Runs the queue; sees every stuck item | HMW2, HMW3 | Morning lead triage and one reassignment |
| Two mainstream BD officers | The everyday case | HMW1 | Chasing a proposal; preparing Thursday's update |
| An Arabic-first colleague (may be one of the above) | Language is not a preference but the working medium | HMW5 | Any task above, run in Arabic |

Eight sessions of 30 to 45 minutes, requested through the ED (`research.md` section 1). Consent, anonymisation and the one-page note per session as in `research.md`.

## 3. Immersion

- **One real site-visit day** with a BD officer, from the car to the debrief: what is written down, where, when, and what is lost between the visit and the CRM. The researcher carries a notebook, no laptop.
- **A guided tour of how each person tracks work today**: the participant opens everything they use in a normal week (spreadsheet, notebook, Teams, WhatsApp, Outlook flags and folders, the Thursday update document) and walks through the last three items on each.

## 4. Artefacts to collect

| Artefact | Why | How it is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The personal spreadsheet or notebook | The real data model people keep | Compared with the record templates; a column people keep that the CRM lacks is a candidate field or a candidate for helper text |
| The last Teams and WhatsApp threads about a deal | How hand-overs and decisions happen today | Message patterns become notification and decision-card copy |
| Outlook flags and folders | How follow-ups are remembered | Baseline for "how follow-ups are tracked" (`research.md` section 7) |
| The Thursday update | What the ED needs formed | The ED's Home and the Thursday digest |
| Three recent chasing messages from the ED | The cost of unformed decisions | Baseline for manager interventions per week |

Artefacts are photographed or copied with client names removed and stored with the session notes, never in the repository.

## 5. Analogous inspiration: three fields where work is handed over without loss

| Field | What to look at | What they do that a CRM does not | Candidate for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Airline operations centre | The shift board and the irregular-operations desk | One board everyone reads; every disruption has an owner, a next action and a time; the handover is a spoken and written briefing against the board, not a search | The ED's Home (Decisions, Escalations, In progress), the Thursday review run from one screen |
| Hospital shift handover | SBAR (situation, background, assessment, recommendation) at the bedside | A fixed order for every handover; the recommendation is part of the handover, not left to the receiver | The Task card order (what, why, expected output, done criteria) and the Decision card (blocked, consequence, options with a recommendation) |
| Dispatch app (ride or delivery) | Offer, accept within a timer, navigate, complete | One offer at a time; accept or decline in two taps; the timer is visible; completion needs proof | Accept within 30 seconds from the notification; the acceptance timer shown on the card; the output link as proof of completion |

Each field gets a one-page note: what we saw, what transfers, what does not (a hospital's stakes and an airline's staffing are not KUEC's), written before synthesis.

## 6. Same-day download

After every session and the immersion day, 30 minutes, the same day:

1. Quotes first, verbatim, in the language spoken.
2. Observations (what the person did), separated from interpretations (what we think it means).
3. Surprises and contradictions with the hypotheses in `research.md`.
4. One line per HMW: evidence for, against, or none.
5. Append to `journal.md` under the date.

## 7. From inspiration to synthesis

End of week 1: cluster the notes into themes by frequency and impact; write five to seven insight statements; one How-might-we per insight; rank by desirability, feasibility and viability; rewrite the personas and the moments that matter from evidence; remove the UNVALIDATED marks only where a named participant supports the statement. The output is the first dated synthesis entry in `journal.md` and the agenda of the first weekly review with the ED.

## 8. What happens if the sessions slip

Design proceeds on the hypotheses in `research.md` and every assumption keeps its UNVALIDATED mark through usability round one. The three screens built this week were built that way, and the scorecard says so (`scorecard.md`, row "usability pass").
